Binary, multiclass, or both?

The 37 SMOTE-family methods in our corpus of 76 papers, sorted by the class setting in which each paper actually generates synthetic data, with an account of how each one builds on SMOTE. All 37 were checked against their PDFs on 28 October 2026.

The reference: SMOTE (2002)

Every method is read against the rule of Chawla et al. (2002): for a minority seed x, pick a partner x_nn among its 5 nearest minority neighbours and create x + λ(x_nn − x) with λ ~ U(0,1). SMOTE fixes six steps in the simplest way (every minority point is a seed, equal points per seed, a uniform partner among 5 neighbours, uniform placement on the segment, no filtering afterwards, run once before training). The table below marks the steps each method changes.

What the categories show

  1. Most SMOTE-family methods generate only for two classes.

    24 of 37 are Binary only. 23 of them evaluate on multiclass sources reduced to two classes: 15 reduce the classes themselves (one class against the rest, merged classes, or two classes kept and the rest dropped) and 8 use ready-made two-class splits such as KEEL's yeast-2_vs_4 without describing the reduction. The remaining one, Cluster-SMOTE, collapses the attack types of its own network data into intrusive vs not. A multiclass name in a dataset list therefore does not mean multiclass generation.

  2. Methods that handle several classes are recent.

    Multiclass only or Binary + Multiclass: 1 of 8 methods from 2002 to 2009, 3 of 10 from 2010 to 2017, and 9 of 19 from 2018 to 2026.

  3. Few methods show both settings, and five never show two classes.

    8 are Binary + Multiclass; 5 are Multiclass only, although their rules would logically cover two classes. Several multiclass extensions of binary methods (ADASYN, KNNOR, QC-SMOTE, CISO) are described or named as future work but never run.

  4. The PDF check corrected how many methods relate to SMOTE.

    The steps changed were corrected for 21 of the 36 variants, mostly by adding steps the earlier coding missed. Papers changing each step: seed selection 24, sample allocation 21, neighbour choice 22, placement rule 13, post-filtering 12, inside a pipeline 8.

SMOTE2002Binary onlyThis paper defines the reference rule and changes none of the six decision points: every minority sample is a seed with N/100 points each (a random N% of seeds when N < 100), the partner is drawn from the k = 5 minority nearest neighbours, and lambda ~ U(0,1) on the segment (Sec 4.2, p.328; algorithm, p.329). Generation runs once before training, combined with random undersampling of the majority (Sec 4.3, p.331). SMOTE-NC changes only the distance used to find neighbours and sets nominal values by a vote of the neighbours (Sec 6.1, p.348).
SMOTEBoost2003Binary + Multiclass●●The generator is unchanged SMOTE (Sec 2, p.109), but it runs inside every AdaBoost.M2 round and the synthetic points are discarded after the round (Sec 3, p.110; Fig. 1, p.111). New points are created by sampling seeds from the round's boosting distribution, which the authors say focuses generation on misclassified or boundary minority cases (pp.110, 116). Allocation, partner, placement and the absence of filtering are as in SMOTE.
SMOTE-Tomek / ENN2004Binary only●SMOTE first balances the training set (Sec. 5, p.25); then Tomek-link pairs are removed from both classes, or, in the ENN variant, every example misclassified by its 3 nearest neighbours is removed from both classes (Sec. 4.3, pp.23-24; Fig. 2). Seeds, neighbours and placement are as in SMOTE; the only addition is cleaning after generation.
Borderline-SMOTE2005Binary only●●●For each minority point its m nearest neighbours in the whole training set are found (Sec. 3, Steps 1-2, p.882); points with only majority neighbours are treated as noise, and points with at least half majority neighbours form the DANGER set, which are the only seeds. In v1 each DANGER seed interpolates towards s of its k = 5 minority neighbours with lambda ~ U(0,1) as in SMOTE (Step 4, p.882). v2 also interpolates towards the seed's nearest majority neighbour with lambda in (0, 0.5), so those points stay near the seed (pp.882-883).
Cluster-SMOTE2006Binary only●k-means is run on the minority examples only and SMOTE is applied separately inside each cluster, after which original and synthetic minority points are merged (Sec. III-C, p.4). The interpolation partner therefore always comes from the seed's own cluster; seeds, the SMOTE rate per cluster and uniform placement on the segment are as in SMOTE (Sec. III-B, p.3; Sec. V-A, p.5).
ADASYN2008Binary only●ADASYN keeps the SMOTE generator: a partner is drawn from the seed's K nearest minority neighbours and the point is seed + lambda(partner - seed), with K = 5 in the experiments (Eq. 5, p.1324; Sec. III-C, p.1325). What changes is allocation: the total G = (ml - ms) beta is shared out as g_i proportional to the share of majority points among each seed's K neighbours (Eqs. 2-4, pp.1323-1324), instead of an equal number per seed.
LS2008Binary only●●LS leaves the SMOTE generator unchanged but applies it per segment: a Hellinger-distance tree of height at most 2 splits the training data into up to 4 segments (Alg 1, Sec 3, pp4-5), and a 5-fold CV wrapper searches each segment for its own undersampling level (0-100%) and SMOTE level (0-500%) (Alg 2, Sec 4, p5). The number of synthetic points per seed therefore varies by segment, and generation runs inside a wrapper search rather than once before training. Seeds, partner and placement inside a segment are as in SMOTE.
Safe-Level-SMOTE2009Binary only●●Each minority point has a safe level, the number of minority points among its k nearest neighbours (Eq 7, p478), and the ratio of the seed's and partner's safe levels bounds the gap (Fig 1, Sec 3, pp478-479): ratio 1 gives U(0,1) as in SMOTE, above 1 pulls the point towards the seed, below 1 towards the partner. When the partner's safe level is 0 the seed is copied, and when both are 0 nothing is generated, so noise pairs are skipped. The partner is still drawn uniformly from the 5 nearest minority neighbours and nothing is removed afterwards.
RAMOBoost2010Binary only●●●The generator is SMOTE's segment interpolation towards one of the seed's k2 nearest minority neighbours (Eq. 1, p.1626; Algorithm 1 step 5). In each boosting round, seeds are drawn with replacement from a ranking that weights each minority point by a logistic function of the number of majority points among its k1 neighbours (steps 2-4, Eqs. 2-3), so border points receive more synthetic samples. Generation is repeated in every AdaBoost.M2 round on a minority subset resampled by the boosting weights (steps 1, 6-10).
SSRBF / DSRBF2011Binary + Multiclass●●The generator is unchanged SMOTE: every original pattern of the chosen class is a seed joined to one of its k = 5 nearest same-class neighbours (Sec. 1, p.1821; Sec. 3.4, p.1826). What changes is which class is oversampled and how many times, set by class size (SSRBF) or by the lowest per-class sensitivity of the current best network (DSRBF). In DSRBF generation runs inside the training loop; SSRBF runs before training.
INOS2013Binary only●●INOS splits the synthetic quota between two generators: 70% from ESPO, a regularised Gaussian of the minority covariance that does not interpolate (Sec. 3.2.2, Eq. 13; r = 0.7, Sec. 4.3), and 30% from ADASYN in a signal space with the null space removed (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 14-15). The ADASYN part allocates points per seed by the share of negatives among its neighbours and interpolates uniformly towards one of the 5 nearest positives, as in SMOTE. Afterwards, Tomek-like noise links between synthetic positives and nearest negatives are removed (Sec. 3.4, pp.5-6).
MWMOTE2014Binary only●●●Seeds are limited to an informative minority set near the borderline majority points, after dropping minority points whose neighbours are all majority (Alg. 1 steps 1-6; Sec. 4.1, p.5). Each seed gets a weight from its closeness to the majority class and its density, and seeds are drawn in proportion to it, so counts per seed vary (Alg. 1 steps 7-9, 12a; Sec. 4.2, pp.6-7). The partner is a random member of the seed's minority cluster from average-linkage clustering, not one of its 5 nearest neighbours (Alg. 1 steps 10, 12b; Secs. 4.3-4.4). Placement is uniform on the segment and nothing is removed afterwards.
RBBoost2015Binary only●●●Each ensemble member gets a training set of the original size with a random class split (Alg. 1 lines 6-7, p.98): the class that must grow is filled with SMOTE (k = 5; Sec. 3, p.97) and the other is randomly undersampled, and when the split is inverted SMOTE grows the original majority class (Alg. 1 lines 12-15; Sec. 4.1, p.98). Partner and placement are as in SMOTE. Generation is redone for every member of a plain or bagging ensemble, or in every AdaBoost.M2 round (RB-Boost, Alg. 2, Sec. 4.2, p.100).
SCUT2015Multiclass only●Synthetic points come from standard SMOTE run inside each under-represented class (Fig. 1); the only change is how many points each class gets, enough to reach the mean class size m with a rate computed per class (Sec. 2). Seeds, partners within the class and placement are as in SMOTE, and generation is a one-off step before training. The EM cluster-based undersampling acts on real points of the large classes, not on synthetic ones.
SMOTE-IPF2015Binary only●Generation is plain SMOTE with k = 5, oversampling to a 50/50 balance (Sec. 3.2, p.189). The only change is a filter applied afterwards: the Iterative-Partitioning Filter, an ensemble of C4.5 models on 9 partitions with majority voting, repeated until fewer than 1% of examples are removed in 3 rounds, which removes noisy original and synthetic examples of both classes (Sec. 3.1, pp.188-189; Sec. 3.3, p.190).
A-SUWO2016Binary only●●●Minority points are grouped by a complete-linkage clustering that will not merge two minority sub-clusters across a majority sub-cluster (Sec. 3.1, pp.26-28). Each sub-cluster's quota is proportional to its cross-validated classification error (Sec. 3.2, Eqs. 3.2-3.3), seeds are drawn with weights favouring closeness to the majority class (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 3.4-3.9), and the partner is one of the seed's nearest neighbours in the same sub-cluster (Alg. 3.1, p.33). Placement stays uniform on the segment (Eq. 3.10).
CGMOS2016Binary only●CGMOS keeps SMOTE's interpolation step unchanged (Sec. 2.3, p.1625). Seeds are drawn in proportion to a weight: the average relative change in kernel-density posterior certainty over all training points if a minority point were added at that seed (Eqs. 5-6, p.1625). Sec. 3 proves that the expected certainty gain is at least that of SMOTE on the training data (Theorem 1).
SMOM2017Multiclass only●SMOM keeps SMOTE-style interpolation between a seed and one of its k1 = 12 neighbours (Supp. Secs. 3, 5). For trapped instances, each neighbour direction gets a selection weight that penalises directions whose segment sphere contains instances of other classes (Algorithm S1; Supp. Sec. 3), so directions are drawn by weight rather than uniformly. NBDOS clustering decides which instances are outstanding (uniform directions) or trapped (weighted directions).
k-means SMOTE2018Binary only●●●k-means clusters the whole input space, and only clusters whose majority-to-minority ratio is below a threshold are kept, so seeds are minority points inside minority-dominated clusters (Sec. 3.1, PDF pp.7-8; Algorithm 1). Samples are shared across kept clusters in proportion to their sparsity, so sparse clusters get more (Sec. 3.1, PDF pp.8-9). Inside each cluster plain SMOTE runs on that cluster's points only, so partners come from the same cluster; placement is unchanged and there is no filtering. With one cluster and no threshold the method reduces to SMOTE (Sec. 3.2, PDF p.9).
G-SMOTE2019Binary only●●Seeds and allocation are SMOTE-like (Sec. 4.1, PDF p.9). The surface point that sets the radius is chosen by a selection strategy: a random minority neighbour as in SMOTE, the nearest majority point, or whichever of the two is closer (Algorithm 1, PDF p.8; Sec. 4.3 step 4). The segment is replaced by a point drawn in a hypersphere around the seed with that radius, truncated and deformed into a hyper-spheroid along the seed-to-surface axis (Sec. 4.2; Sec. 4.3 steps 6-9, PDF pp.14-16). With full truncation and deformation and the minority strategy the region becomes SMOTE's segment (Sec. 5.4, PDF p.24).
GAMO2019Binary + Multiclass●●●●●GAMO creates each sample as a convex combination of all training points of a minority class (Eq. 1, Sec. 3.2, p.1697), not as a point on a seed-neighbour segment, so there is no single seed and no k-NN partner: a generator network learns the weights adversarially to place points near class peripheries that the classifier gets wrong. Generation runs inside end-to-end training that alternates generator, classifier and discriminator updates (Alg. 1, p.1698), usually in a learned feature space.
AB-SMOTE2020Binary only●●As in Borderline-SMOTE, only danger minority points (at least half but not all of their M nearest neighbours are majority) are seeds (Sec. II.B, p.32; Sec. III, pp.32-33). The difference is that the k nearest neighbours, and so the partner, are searched only within the borderline set (Sec. III, Figs. 5-6, p.33). Placement is P'i + gap (P'j - P'i) with gap in [0,1], as in SMOTE (Sec. III, p.33).
EMDO2021Multiclass only●●●●Each minority class is covered by Mahalanobis ellipsoids and its quota is split across them by inverse density times purity (Eqs. 32-34, p.11). In pure ellipsoids points are drawn along the ellipsoid axes without interpolation (Eqs. 37-40). In impure ellipsoids a modified Borderline-SMOTE interpolates: only borderline points are seeds, and the partner comes from their 5 Mahalanobis neighbours within the ellipsoid (Eqs. 41-42, p.12).
HDE2021Binary only●●●The partner is one of the k = 5 nearest majority instances (nearest enemies), found with a mix of Euclidean distance and how often earlier ensemble members disagree on the two points (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 3-4, pp.6-7). The step is x + r(x_i - x) with r drawn from N(0,1), so points cluster near the seed and can fall on either side of it (Eq. 5, p.8). Generation runs inside every member of a sequential bagging ensemble (Secs. 3.1-3.4).
RSMOTE2021Binary + Multiclass●●Minority points whose k neighbours are all majority are dropped as noise, and the remaining seeds are split into borderline and safe groups by 2-means on their relative density (Defs. 1-3, pp.401-402; Algorithm 1, p.403). Each group gets a share matching its size, and within a group each seed gets a weight (k-m)/(k+1), so safer seeds produce more points (Step 3, p.402). Points are made with SMOTE's own routine, uniform on the segment, once before training (Algorithm 1 line 22).
FW-SMOTE2022Binary only●The only change is how the k nearest minority neighbours are found: Euclidean distance is replaced by a feature-weighted Minkowski distance whose weights come from a supervised feature ranking, and low-ranked features can be dropped from the distance (Eqs. 4-5, 9-12, pp.4-5; Algorithm 1 steps 2-9). Seeds, the number per seed, the random partner and placement on the segment are as in SMOTE (Algorithm 1 steps 10-14), and the method reduces to SMOTE with equal weights and p = 2 (p.6).
KNNOR2022Binary only●●●●Seeds are only the densest d% of minority points, ranked by distance to their k-th minority neighbour, with d chosen automatically (Alg. 1, Sec. 3.1, pp.3-4). Instead of one random partner, the point moves step by step through all k nearest minority neighbours, each step bounded by the smallest majority-minority distance (Eq. 5, Alg. 2, Sec. 3.2, pp.6-8). A candidate is kept only if all its k nearest neighbours are minority points (Alg. 3, Sec. 3.3, p.8).
SVDD-WSMOTE2022Binary only●●●●Noisy or overlapped minority points inside the majority SVDD sphere are removed before generation (Secs. 4.1-4.2). Each remaining seed gets a number of points proportional to a weight combining closeness to the boundary and sparsity (Eqs. 21-24), and the partner is drawn from its 5 nearest neighbours in proportion to their weights (Eq. 25, p.25). Placement is SMOTE's; synthetic points that fall inside the SVDD boundary are rejected and regenerated, and any still missing are filled by copying the seed (Alg. steps 36-52, pp.26-27).
GLOS2023Multiclass only provisional●●●●Only one of GLOS's three generators interpolates: AMBO, used for borderline seeds (Alg. 2, p.2076). Its partner is any instance inside a radius-R sphere, possibly of another class, and the step is capped at the seed's nearest-neighbour distance rather than covering the whole segment (Alg. 2 line 12). The number of points per seed comes from discreteness and is split across classes by their discreteness ratios. The other two generators place points on Mahalanobis contours (MMO) or at random within a radius (RRO).
NROMM2023Binary + Multiclass●●●●●Clustering splits minority samples into inland, borderline and trapped (Sec. 3.2). Inland and borderline seeds are interpolated, inland ones with a partner from the same cluster and borderline ones with a nearby majority neighbour; trapped seeds get points from an optimised embedding, not interpolation (Sec. 3.3, Eqs. 2-4). The number of points per seed follows (1 - local density) times the class-size gap, categorical values come from a VDM-constrained set near the seed (Sec. 4.1), and borderline and trapped majority samples are removed afterwards.
OREM / OREM-M2023Binary + Multiclass●●●●●●OREM keeps every minority point as a seed with equal counts (Alg. 1). It replaces the k-NN partner set with 'assistant seeds' from a candidate region that grows until q = 5 consecutive majority points are met, keeping a candidate only if the sphere on the seed-candidate segment holds no majority point, so borderline majority points can be partners (Alg. 1; Sec. 3.1.2). The point is x_i + g(x_s - x_i) with g ~ U[0,1], halved when the partner is a majority point. OREM-M adds class-quota sampling and a nearest-neighbour rejection filter (Alg. 2), and OREMBoost runs the generator inside each AdaBoost.M2 round (Alg. 3).
MC-MBRC2024Binary + Multiclass●●●●Seeds are only the safe samples of each class, after membership-based noise removal (Alg. 1; Sec. 3). The partner is one of the 5 same-class neighbours among the safe and border samples, and placement is SMOTE's x_i + U(0,1)(x_j - x_i) (Alg. 2). Each seed gets a random number of points up to the class quota divided by the number of safe seeds. Afterwards, a CCR-style energy sphere moves overlapping and generated samples of larger classes towards their class centre (Algs. 3-4, p.6).
MKC-SMOTE2024Multiclass only●●●●A minority point is a seed only if at least half of its 5 nearest neighbours, taken from all classes, share its class (Sec. III step 2; Alg. 1). Instead of a random minority partner, the method uses the centroid of the seed's neighbours and places new points on the line from that centroid through the seed, possibly beyond the seed (Sec. III steps 3-5; Alg. 1 lines 23-26). It generates twice the needed number and keeps the candidates nearest the centroid (Sec. III step 6, p.5).
GDHS2025Binary + Multiclass●●●●Generation interpolates between a minority seed and one of its 5 same-class neighbours (Eq. 3, p.3), but each seed-direction pair gets a weight that favours safe seeds (same-class share among neighbours, Eq. 2) and directions whose synthetic region holds few other-class points (Eqs. 5-10). Each minority class is filled to the mean class size by weighted random draws rather than a fixed number per seed (Alg. 1). Afterwards, original majority points in the synthetic regions, or overlapping other majority classes, are removed (Sec. 3.2, Eqs. 11-16).
CISO2026Binary only●●●●Anchors are drawn with replacement in proportion to a safety score, so each anchor gets a varying number of points (Eqs. 8-9, p.8). Two different minority neighbours of the anchor are drawn with weights from their clearance and a q-Gaussian locality kernel (Eqs. 10-11). The new point lies on the segment between those two neighbours, not between anchor and neighbour, with lambda drawn from a truncated q-Gaussian instead of U(0,1) (Eqs. 12-14, p.12). Nothing is removed afterwards. Running time is 39 times SMOTE's at the median (Sec. 6.7, p.29).
Loyalty-SMOTE2026Binary only provisional●●●Generation is plain SMOTE (k = 5) applied only to boundary minority samples whose Loyalty, an inverse-distance-weighted balance of same-class and other-class neighbours, lies between 0 and m (Sec. 3.3). Samples with negative Loyalty are treated as possible noise and kept out of generation, so the partner pool is also restricted. Afterwards Loyalty is recomputed: flagged minority samples still below 0 are removed and flagged majority samples are removed by an LOF test (Sec. 3.4, Eqs. 10-12).
QC-SMOTE2026Binary only●●●●Seeds are drawn in proportion to a trust score combining safe level, minority density and distance from the majority class (Eqs. 2-6, Sec. 3.2, p.5), and the number of attempts per seed also follows trust (Eq. 7, Sec. 3.3, p.6). Each candidate is still x_i + lambda(x_j - x_i) with a 5-NN minority partner, but lambda ~ U(0, alpha_r) shrinks with overlap and the best of K_r candidates is kept by a purity and clearance score (Eqs. 8-18, Secs. 3.4-3.5, pp.6-7). A candidate that fails the thresholds is replaced by a copy of the most trusted point near the seed (Eqs. 19-21, Sec. 3.6, p.7).

Decisions still open

Loyalty-SMOTE: Binary only or Binary + Multiclass?

It runs on 5 multiclass datasets, but only the smallest class is ever oversampled and results use binary metrics. Coded Binary only for now, following rule 1.

GLOS: Multiclass only or Binary + Multiclass?

Its only two-class run is a parameter-tuning demo on Sonar (Fig. 7), whose class count the paper does not state. Coded Multiclass only for now.

Decided: supplementary experiments count

Experiments reported only in a paper's supplement count, because they show what the method can do. GAMO is therefore Binary + Multiclass (its two-class runs are tabular, in the supplement).

OREM / OREM-M: one entry with two variants

OREM is run on two-class data and OREM-M on multiclass data; OREMBoost is run on both. Coded Binary + Multiclass for the paper as a whole.

NROMM: Core or Partial?

Seeds of the "trapped" type get points from an optimised embedding, not interpolation. Still coded Core.

SMOM: wrong PDF in Drive

The file named as the SMOM paper contains INOS. SMOM was coded from its supplement; re-check once the main PDF is in Drive.
