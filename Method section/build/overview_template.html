SMOTE Family at 25

What twenty-five years of methods built on SMOTE have changed, and in which class setting they work. SMOTE-family methods from a corpus of papers, 2002 to 2026.

Every result on this page rests on four definitions. They were fixed before the papers were coded, and every paper was coded against them.

The corpus: SMOTE25 core corpus

The papers analysed here are a curated collection the team assembled in a shared folder with four sub-folders (binary, multiclass, both, surveys), not the result of a systematic database search. The folder holds 100 files: 12 supplements and 88 paper files, 12 of them second copies of a paper filed under two settings. Merging the copies leaves distinct papers from 1972 to 2026, so is a count of what the collection contains, not a target.

The reference: SMOTE (Chawla et al., 2002) and its six decision points

For a minority seed xi, pick a partner xj among its five nearest minority neighbours and place a new point at a random position on the segment between them. SMOTE-family

A method is SMOTE-family when it creates synthetic minority examples with SMOTE's equation, xnew = xi + λ(xj − xi) with λ ~ U(0, 1), or with a rule that extends it in one of four ways. It may change any of the six decision points and still belong to the family. The definition concerns the generator, not the method's name.

A different law for λλ ≤ 0.5, a shifted or Gaussian λ (Borderline-SMOTE2, Safe-Level-SMOTE, HDE, CISO)
A region around the segmentthe point is drawn near the segment, not on it (G-SMOTE)
Extrapolation along the lineλ outside [0, 1] (MKC-SMOTE, HDE)
More than two instancesxnew = Σ αm xm, αm ≥ 0, Σ αm = 1 (GAMO, KNNOR)

Outside the family: sampling around one point without a partner (sphere, Mahalanobis contour, random direction) and drawing from a fitted or learned distribution.

Core
Interpolation is the main generator, alone or inside a hybrid, cleaning or ensemble procedure.
Partial
Interpolation is one of several generators (INOS, EMDO, GLOS).
Not counted
Related to SMOTE, but generates in another way (spheres, Mahalanobis contours, potential fields) or not at all.
Study
Evaluates or reviews SMOTE without proposing a generator. Not counted.

SMOTE-family = Core + Partial = papers. The other papers in the corpus have no direct link to SMOTE.

Three class categories

A method is classified by what its paper shows its generator doing, not by what it could do in principle or by the names in its dataset list.

Binary only

Every experiment in which the generator creates synthetic data is a two-class problem.

A multiclass dataset reduced to two classes (one class against the rest, merged classes, a KEEL split such as yeast-2_vs_4).A multiclass extension that is described but never run.

Multiclass only

The generator creates synthetic data with three or more classes kept, and is never run on a two-class problem.

A rule that would cover two classes in principle but is not shown on them.

Binary + Multiclass

The same generator is run on at least one two-class problem and on at least one problem with three or more classes kept, without reducing it first.

Experiments reported only in supplementary material.

Other data types

A paper that generates more than one type of data (tables, images, time series) is classified for each data type separately; its overall category combines them. SMOTE-family papers use non-tabular data (INOS, GAMO, KNNOR).

Provisional cases

Two categories await a team decision: Loyalty-SMOTE (runs on multiclass data but oversamples only the smallest class; coded Binary only) and GLOS (its only two-class run is a parameter-tuning demo; coded Multiclass only). They are drawn with dashed edges.

Our position (draft)

Stop proposing new SMOTE variants as a contribution in themselves

Twenty-five years of variants have re-decided the same six choices, each new method changes more of them at once, costs more to run, and is judged mostly by its own authors against plain SMOTE. Several independent studies now find that rebalancing adds little once a strong learner, a tuned threshold or a calibrated model is used, and that it can harm risk estimates. The marginal value of variant number 38 is low. Where imbalance must be handled, the evidence points to better baselines and better evaluation rather than a new generator.

Why: what the papers report

Synthetic points fall among the majority

Preprint · in corpus

Hassanat et al. (2022), Stop Oversampling for Class Imbalance Learning: after hiding part of the majority class, synthetic minority points from 72 oversamplers, mostly SMOTE-based, often lay closer to the hidden majority points; the authors advise avoiding oversampling. Evidence is narrow (three Yeast datasets and Vehicle3).

The same holds on medical data

Verified

Hassanat et al. (2023, IEEE ISCC): on medical datasets many synthetic samples fall outside the minority class.

No gain in discrimination, worse calibration

Verified

van den Goorbergh et al. (2022, JAMIA): random over- and undersampling and SMOTE gave clinical logistic models no better AUC but systematically overestimated minority-class risk. Carriero et al. (2025, Statistics in Medicine) find the same for machine-learning models, and Piccininni et al. (2024) report the calibration cost of random resampling.

Strong learners do not need it

Preprint

Elor and Averbuch-Elor (2022): balancing helps weak learners but not gradient boosting with a tuned decision threshold; they find gains only in some tuned-F1 settings.

High dimensions

Verified

Blagus and Lusa (2013): SMOTE shrinks variance and adds correlation in high-dimensional data and does worse than undersampling there unless variables are selected first.

Many reported gains were leakage

Verified

Santos et al. (2018) show that oversampling before cross-validation inflates results; Vandewiele et al. (2021) found more than 20 preterm-birth studies whose near-perfect scores collapsed once the data were split first.

Results depend on the measure and the learner

Verified

Tantithamthavorn et al. (2020): the effect of rebalancing depends on the evaluation measure and the classifier, and it changes which features a model treats as important.

Already in 2002, SMOTE's own C4.5 AUC gains over undersampling were all below 0.01, without a significance test. New methods beat SMOTE in 420 of 541 per-dataset comparisons printed in their own papers, under their own protocols (Data section).

So what should the field do?

Stop

  • Proposing a new interpolation variant whose only evidence is a win over plain SMOTE in its own paper.
  • Oversampling before splitting the data, or outside the training folds.
  • Reporting accuracy or ROC-AUC alone on imbalanced data.
  • Calling a method multiclass when it was only run on data reduced to two classes.

Keep, with care

  • Plain SMOTE as a baseline, not as a default preprocessing step.
  • Oversampling for weak learners, where the evidence still shows gains.
  • Oversampling inside a pipeline only when it is compared against the same pipeline without it.

Start

  • Compare against class weighting and threshold tuning with a strong learner such as gradient boosting.
  • Report PR-AUC and a calibration measure next to ROC-AUC, and per-dataset results.
  • Change one decision point at a time and report the ablation.
  • State the class setting and how any multiclass data were reduced.
  • Report running time relative to SMOTE.

Read this as a position, not a finding. The evidence above comes from the team's development brief (each source labelled Verified or Preprint) and from our own coding; two of the strongest statements are preprints. In the manuscript the interpretation must be written by P, and the claim should be limited to what these studies tested: tabular data, mostly binary problems, and the learners they used.

Our part of the paper answers one question: what have twenty-five years of methods built on SMOTE actually changed, and in which class setting do they work? A reader should leave with three messages and one way of reading any new oversampler.

  1. Twenty-five years of SMOTE variants re-decide six choices made in 2002.

    Every one of the variants changes some of the same six decision points: seeds, allocation, partner, placement, filtering, and where generation runs. The most changed are seed selection (), the partner () and allocation (). One question from 2002, an adaptive choice of the neighbourhood size k, is not addressed by any method in our coding (still to be confirmed against the papers).

  2. The family is mostly binary, and dataset lists hide it.

    of methods only ever generate for two classes. of them evaluate on multiclass sources reduced to two classes ( by their own reduction, through ready-made splits). Multiclass extensions are often described but rarely run. Methods able to handle several classes grew from before 2010 to since 2018.

  3. New variants change more, cost more, and prove less.

    The mean number of decisions changed per variant rose from (2002 to 2009) to (2018 to 2026); recent methods cost tens of times SMOTE's running time; few test one change at a time. Meanwhile, generators in the corpus have left interpolation altogether, of them since 2018.

How to read a new oversampler

  • Which of the six decisions does it change?
  • Is each change tested on its own?
  • In which class setting is it actually run, and were classes reduced?
  • What does it cost compared with SMOTE?
  • Does it beat class weighting and threshold tuning with a strong learner?

What we do not claim

  • That oversampling never helps: the evidence is for tabular data and the learners tested.
  • Which method performs best: performance comparisons belong to the Performance Metrics and Evaluation sections.
  • Anything about deep generative oversamplers beyond their position outside the family.

How it connects to the team's sections

  • Datasets: most binary-only methods test on reduced multiclass data; the same dataset name can hide different splits.
  • Performance metrics: multiclass methods use macro or MAUC metrics, Loyalty-SMOTE uses binary ones; strong baselines and calibration decide whether oversampling is worth it.
