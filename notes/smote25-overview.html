25 Years of SMOTE: how it developed, and where it is used

The SMOTE25 survey tells a development story in three parts. First, SMOTE as published in 2002, read on three angles: the data it assumed, the algorithm it proposed and the evidence it offered. Second, what twenty-five years of work added along each angle. Third, where SMOTE-based methods reached real applications, and how strong that evidence is.

            From the 2002 starting point to today

            The imbalance ratio was never the whole story

            Batista, Prati and Monard (2004) found that class imbalance alone did not systematically hurt their learners; the trouble came from having few minority examples together with complicating factors such as class overlap. Twenty years later, Naglik and Lango (2024) open GMMSampling with the same argument: the ratio is not the main difficulty, its combination with overlap and minority sub-concepts is.

            Modality is a scope decision

            What the data lens asks of the paper

            • Add a difficulty-factor column to the extraction sheet and code it from the full text. The codes on this page come from abstracts and will miss factors that only appear in the experiments.
            • For each benchmark the survey cites, record at least the imbalance ratio and one overlap measure, so that claims such as robust to overlap can be checked against the data rather than taken from the abstract.
            • Decide the modality scope explicitly. Either state that SMOTE25 covers tabular data, or add a search for image, text and time-series oversampling.

            Where methods change the pipeline

            Read as a pipeline, SMOTE makes four choices: which minority points seed new ones, whom each seed pairs with, how the new point is produced, and what is cleaned afterwards. Two more stages sit around it: inside a learner, and across several minority classes. Most works change more than one stage, so the taxonomy has to allow a work in several places and say so, the issue Pradipta et al. (2021) left unannounced.

            Invention moved to the generation operator

            All 73 works

            What the algorithm lens asks of the paper

            • Write an inclusion rule before the search, for example: a method that generates or removes training examples to counter imbalance, or a study that evaluates such methods. Keep context works such as ENN, Tomek links, AdaBoost and CTGAN as background, outside the counts.
            • Use a faceted taxonomy with three declared facets (role, stage, operator) and allow multiple stages per work. Report the overlaps instead of hiding them.
            • Fill tables/variants-summary.tex at two levels, as the survey analysis proposes: one row per family (idea, advantages, limitations, representative works) and one row per method.

            What the evaluation lens asks of the paper

            • Re-score every work from its full text on four items: where resampling happens relative to the split, which metrics, which statistical test, and whether code is released. The markers here are a screening pass.
            • If SMOTE25 runs its own comparison: resample inside the training folds only, use at least three classifiers including gradient boosting, report PR-AUC, MCC and G-mean with their variation, use a Friedman-type test with post-hoc correction, and publish seeds and code. These are the experiment items of JAIR's reproducibility checklist.
            • Consider a fourth research question, how strong is the evidence, because this lens is where the survey can add something that Fernández et al. (2018) did not.
