\paragraph{Which data sets could anchor a cross-family comparison.}
Table~\ref{tab:data-candidates} lists the names that the task named for
inspection together with the names that pass two written criteria: use by
at least ten papers (30 names), or use by three to nine papers with every printed size of
at least 10,000 instances, a range that no entry of the frequent set reaches.
No name used by three or more papers is printed with 100 or more features in
every paper. IR is not used for the selection because its definition varies
between papers.
\claudedraft{The frequently used benchmarks are small: among the entries that
print a size, half have 800 or fewer instances and half nine or fewer features. Names printed with
10,000 or more instances in every paper are used by three to nine papers each,
so a comparison across method families that wants to cover size has to look
beyond the frequent set.}

\paragraph{Limits of the evidence.}
Size, dimensionality and the number of classes are printed for 80.2\%, 75.9\% and 77.4\% of the
1,653 named entries; class proportions in some form for 56.8\%.
A measured class overlap exists for 36 entries in two papers, on two
incompatible scales, and the curated evidence contains no statement about sparsity.
\claudedraft{The corpus therefore cannot show which ranges of overlap, noise
or sparsity the benchmarks cover; candidates in Table~\ref{tab:data-candidates}
are chosen on size and use only.}
