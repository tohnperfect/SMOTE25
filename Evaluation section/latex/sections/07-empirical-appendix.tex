\section{Illustrative Runs and the Coding of the Studies}
\label{sec:empirical-appendix}

\paragraph{Illustrative runs.} Section~\ref{sec:empirical} uses one small experiment in two places: to show how resampling affects different measures (Figure~\ref{fig:empirical-smote-vs-none}) and to show what a recommended results table looks like (Table~\ref{tab:empirical-example}). We resampled 110 binary imbalanced datasets of the KEEL repository~\parencite{alcalafdez2011keel} with no resampling, SMOTE~\parencite{chawla2002smote}, MWMOTE~\parencite{barua2014mwmote}, CISO~\parencite{yadav2025ciso} and QC-SMOTE~\parencite{upman2026qcsmote}, and trained five learners on the result: CART, k-NN ($k = 5$), an SVM with an RBF kernel, logistic regression and a random forest, with the same fixed scikit-learn settings for every sampler. Performance was estimated by one run of stratified 5-fold cross-validation with fixed seeds and the same folds for every sampler and learner; oversampling and feature scaling were fitted on the training folds only, and ROC-AUC and PR-AUC were computed from predicted probabilities or, for the SVM, from the decision function. Every sampler ran with the settings recommended by its authors; CISO and QC-SMOTE are our own re-implementations. These runs illustrate the form of a report and the effect of resampling on different measures; with a single cross-validation run and no class-weighting baseline they say nothing about the merit of any sampler \TODO{repeat the cross-validation at least five times if the runs stay in the paper}.
